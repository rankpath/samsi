## 1. The two anchors

**Pantone Colour of the Year 2026 — Cloud Dancer (PANTONE 11-4201)**
≈ `#F0EEE9`. The first white ever chosen. Pantone frames it as a blank canvas and
a "whisper of calm in a noisy world" — explicitly a response to overstimulation,
and explicitly linked to the open frontier of AI. Not cool, not warm: balanced.

For web this matters more than any accent trend. It legitimises the shift already
underway from `#FFFFFF` to soft mineral off-whites as the default canvas.

**WGSN x Coloro Colour of the Year 2027 — Luminous Blue (Coloro 125-28-38)**
≈ `#1E3FCB`. A vivid cobalt, close to lapis lazuli. WGSN's strategist describes it
as "the bridge between nature and technology" and a gender-inclusive,
transseasonal shade that works across fashion and consumer tech surfaces.
Announced April 2025, so by 2027 it is a mature trend, not a risky bet.

The pairing is the whole story: **a quiet neutral canvas carrying one confident,
saturated signal colour.** That is also, conveniently, the structure of a good
interface.

## 4. What this means on screen

Fashion forecasts don't transfer directly. Translation rules:

- **A forecast colour at full saturation is almost never body text.** Luminous
  Blue on Cloud Dancer is roughly 7:1 — fine. Maize on white is around 1.9:1 —
  decorative only.
- **Trend colours belong on 5–10% of the screen.** The trend is the accent and
  the neutral, not the whole palette.
- **Warm vs cool neutral is the fastest way to date a design.** Cool grey
  (`#F5F6F8`) reads 2019 SaaS. Mineral off-white (`#F0EEE9`, `#F8F5F1`) reads
  2026–27.
- **Tint, don't invent.** Need a soft background for a section? Use the accent at
  6–10% opacity over the neutral rather than adding a new hue.

## 5. Digital-native trends the fashion forecast misses

These come from UI practice rather than colour forecasting, and matter more for
a website than the runway colours do:

1. **Elevated neutrals.** Soft grey, warm sand, stone, oatmeal, muted clay
   replacing stark white interfaces. Reported to reduce screen fatigue and make
   products feel human rather than institutional.
2. **Soft gradients 2.0.** Light passing through glass, not rainbow mesh. Low
   chroma range, blurred edges, used on hero surfaces only.
3. **Mood mode.** Dark mode as a designed palette with its own tokens, rather
   than an algorithmic inversion. Often a hued dark surface plus a lifted accent.
4. **Neon as micro-accent.** Tiny doses on dark surfaces in developer and
   AI-tooling products. A 2px focus ring, a status dot — not a background.
5. **Polished brutalism.** High-contrast borders and heavy type survive, but the
   deliberately ugly colour clashes have been replaced by disciplined
   complementary pairs.
6. **Adaptive / context-aware colour.** Palettes that shift with time of day,
   content, or user state. Only worth building if the token layer is clean.
7. **Accessibility as a design constraint, not a retrofit.** Focus states,
   non-colour status cues, and contrast budgets decided at palette time.
