# Palettes by website category

Eighteen ready systems, each tuned to the 2026–27 direction: a mineral neutral
canvas, ink with real weight, and one accent that carries the conversion.

Every contrast ratio below was computed, not estimated. Where an accent is too
light for white text, the **button text colour is specified** — that is the fix,
not a different accent.

How to read each row: `surface` / `ink` / `ink-muted` / `accent` / `accent-quiet`.
`accent-quiet` is the accent at roughly 10% over the surface — use it for
selected rows, badges, and section bands.

---

## 1. B2B SaaS
`#F7F6F3` / `#12151C` / `#5B6072` / `#1E3FCB` / `#E4E7F7`
Ink 16.9:1 · muted 5.8:1 · white on accent 8.0:1.
Luminous Blue does the job navy used to, with more energy. Warm-grey surface
stops it feeling like every other dashboard. Accent on CTAs and active nav only.

## 2. AI products and developer tools
`#101317` / `#E9EBE6` / `#8C9299` / `#B8E62E` / `#1C2418`
Ink 15.5:1 · muted 5.9:1 · **dark text `#101317` on the lime, 12.8:1**.
Dark-first with a single acid accent. Never white text on lime. Keep the lime
to focus rings, active states, and one CTA per screen.

## 3. Healthcare and medical clinics
`#F6F4F0` / `#16211E` / `#5A6663` / `#1B6B63` / `#DCEAE7`
Ink 15.1:1 · muted 5.4:1 · white on accent 6.3:1.
Deep teal reads clinical-but-human and avoids the "spa" trap of pale mint.
Older patients need the 16:1 ink — don't soften body text to grey.

## 4. Aesthetic and cosmetic surgery clinics
`#F7F2EE` / `#231A18` / `#6B5B54` / `#8B4A33` / `#EEE1DA`
Ink 15.3:1 · muted 5.8:1 · white on accent 6.7:1.
Russet instead of gold. Gold gradients signal "luxury claim", which is exactly
what advertising regulators scrutinise. Warm, expensive, quiet.

## 5. E-commerce and retail
`#FBF8F4` / `#1F1A17` / `#6A605A` / `#EF5A24` / `#FBE6DB`
Ink 16.3:1 · muted 5.8:1. Energy Orange at full value takes **dark text
`#1F1A17` on the button (5.1:1)**; if you need white button text, drop the
accent to `#C2410C` (5.2:1). Reserve orange for add-to-cart and price urgency.

## 6. Luxury, jewellery, fine dining
`#1E3A2C` / `#EDEFE9` / `#A9B3A6` / `#EBB13C` / `#2A4A39`
Ink 10.7:1 · muted 5.7:1 · **deep green text on the maize accent, 6.4:1**.
Deep Green as the new black, with maize as metal. Never white on maize.

## 7. Fintech and banking
`#F5F6F8` / `#0E1726` / `#5C6678` / `#1E3FCB` / `#E3E7F6`
Ink 16.6:1 · muted 5.4:1 · white on accent 8.0:1.
The one category where a cooler neutral is still correct — it reads as precision.
Positive `#137A4B`, critical `#B3261E`, and never rely on hue alone for a balance.

## 8. Education and courses

Ink 16.3:1 · muted 6.3:1 · white on accent 5.7:1.

## 9. Wellness, yoga, fitness

Ink 14.1:1 · muted 5.6:1 · white on accent 6.2:1.
Patina blue-green rather than sage — sage has become the default and now reads
as a template. Avoid pure white; the whole point is a softer surface.

## 10. Food, restaurants, cafés

Appetite colours are warm and slightly dirty, not neon. Let photography carry

## 11. Travel and hospitality

Deep sea blue, not sky blue. Sky blue on white always fails contrast and ends up

## 12. Real estate and architecture

## 13. Creative agencies and portfolios

Ink 15.9:1 · muted 5.8:1 · white on accent 5.6:1.
Hued dark surface, never `#000`. If you want the neon look, add a second accent

`#FFF9EF` / `#26201A` / `#6B6057` / `#26688F` / `#DCE8EF`
Ink 15.4:1 · muted 5.8:1 · white on accent 6.1:1.
Bright colours belong in illustration, not in the interface chrome. Parents are

## Cross-category rules

- **Positive / critical defaults** that pass on any light surface above:
  positive `#137A4B` (white text 4.9:1), critical `#B3261E` (white text 6.9:1).
- **Dark-mode conversion**: lift the accent 10–15% in lightness, set the surface
  to a hued dark (`#12151C`, `#1E3A2C`, `#12101A`), and re-check every pair.
  A palette that passes in light mode does not automatically pass in dark.
- **If two categories overlap** (a clinic that sells products, an agency that
  runs courses), take the surface and ink from the more conservative category and
  the accent from the more commercial one.
